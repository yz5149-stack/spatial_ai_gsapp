            How it answers the brief

            A spatial simulation and an environment-design tool. The environment is a convenience store generated from a short genome; two kinds of agents live in it; the designer edits, compares and evolves the store by how the agents perform. The full semantic model is in SEMANTIC_MODEL.md.

            RequirementIn the simulation
            Baseline from a semantic modelEvery store is built from a genome: front door, stockroom door, coolers, counter, gondolas (position, length, direction, 54″ or 72″, products, aisle sign), cartons and floor zones.
            Two notions of locationMetric (x, y in metres, and heights in 3D) · store address (“G3 · west face · bay 2”, “Cooler aisle”, “Register”) · relative to an agent’s own landmarks (“2 m E, 1.5 m N of D1”). Hover over the plan to see all three.
            Two notions of distanceWalking path (Dijkstra, no corner cutting) · acoustic (straight 3D line + muffling per cell of shelf, cooler, wall; low shelves under the ear line don’t muffle) · effort in seconds (wet floor slows) · straight-line.
            Two kinds of agentsSurvivors (shopping list, flashlight cone, read shelves and signs) and the creature (vision cone, hearing).
            Limited knowledge, scanningEach agent starts with an empty map and fills it from its cone. Rays pass over anything below its eye: standing, you see over a 54″ gondola; crouching, you don’t. Dark floor only shows itself up close.
            Spatial memory, landmarksSurvivors: items, aisle signs, dead ends, dark corners, hiding places, where they saw the creature. Creature: dead ends, hiding places, footsteps, catches, and hunting grounds that persist across nights, so it patrols where survivors tend to be.
            Goals and performanceSurvivors: escape with the list (escaped, items, time, exposure). Creature: catch (catch rate, time to first catch, and its learning across nights).
            Planning aheadSurvivors try every order of the items they know the places of, plus the walk out, and take the shortest. When frightened they pick a refuge they reach well before the creature, out of its sight at crouching height. The creature plans a sweep of the hiding places it remembers, and a patrol through its strongest hunting grounds.
            EnvironmentPhysical (a real-size store), partially observable, multi-agent (survivors block each other), dynamic (agents move, the sun moves), non-deterministic (seeded randomness: arrivals, lists, slips).
            ObstaclesGondolas, coolers, counter, cartons, walls.
            Hindrances and affordancesWet floor (slow, loud slips) · crushed bags (louder steps) · lights out (hard to see, easy to hide) · rubber mats (quiet) · aisle signs (read a whole aisle from 6 m) · daylight through the glass (see farther).
            Generate environments: manualChoose: two variations run side by side; keep one and two more grow from it. Plus direct editing on the plan.
            Generate environments: automatedEvolve: a population scored by the agents’ aggregate performance, mutated over a chosen number of epochs, with a chart of best and mean scores.
            Real-world data, 3D + timeThe storefront faces south on a street in Morningside Heights. The sun’s real position for the chosen hour is cast through the glazing (sill 0.45 m, head 2.45 m), past the real shelf heights, onto the floor; hourly weather from the Open-Meteo archive (the 2016 blizzard) or live forecast sets how many survivors come, how far sound carries, and whether hand warmers are on every list.

            Real measurements used

            Gondolas 54″ (1.37 m) and 72″ (1.83 m); reach-in coolers 2.03 m; counter 40″ (1.02 m); aisles at least 0.9 m (36″); grid cells 0.5 m; the store 18 × 13 m (about 2,500 sq ft). Eye heights 1.6 m standing, 0.95 m crouching.
