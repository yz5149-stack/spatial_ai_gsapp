      How the agents work

      1–2 · Little knowledge, a cone of vision

      Both agents spawn knowing only the cells around them. They see along a cone (the creature 100°, 8 cells, red; the survivor 110°, 6 cells, a flashlight). Rays stop at the first tree, rock or bush. Everything they know is drawn on their own map.

      3 · Obstacles and other agents

      They plan only through ground they have seen. The survivor gives the creature a wide berth: cells near where it last saw or heard it cost more, and it runs from it.

      4 · Goals and how they are measured

      Creature, phase 1: map the forest, measured by coverage and cells seen per step. Phase 2: catch the survivor. Survivor: reach the hospital, measured by time and steps.

      5 · Mapping (like a robot vacuum)

      The creature always walks to the nearest frontier (seen ground next to unseen ground), preferring what is ahead, so it sweeps a corridor to its end before turning. The survivor does the same, biased east.

      6 · Landmarks

      A dead end (closed on three sides), a clearing (open on all sides), the hospital door, footsteps heard, the creature seen. Each is stored relative to the previous one: “D3: 6 E, 4 S of C1”.

      7 · Using its landmarks

      On hearing a sound, the creature plans the shortest route on its own map. When it loses the trail it checks the dead ends it remembers nearby, because that is where a survivor gets trapped. The survivor runs straight for the door once it has seen it, and never flees into a dead end it has marked.

      8 · One agent’s spatial knowledge

      The two maps above are exactly what each agent knows: seen and unseen ground, its trajectory, its landmarks, its current route.

      9–10 · Many agents, together

      Rounds repeat with new survivors and the creature keeps its memory. +20 rounds runs them at once; the overlays show all survivors’ paths and a heatmap of where they were heard.
